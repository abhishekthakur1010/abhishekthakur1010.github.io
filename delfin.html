04Working with constraint

Discovery and delivery had to happen together.

No room for a long research phase. Understanding came from conversations with fleet owners and drivers, product and technical inputs, and rapid validation during execution.

Patterns we designed around
"Tell me what needs attention before my truck gets affected."
"Don't make me understand how every bank works."
"Let me continue where I left off."
"Give me one place to manage services across my vehicles."

Synthesised needs, not survey statistics — the signals we consistently heard and designed toward.

Design principle 02

Two banks. Two wallet models. One mental model for the fleet owner.

IDFC keeps a balance per FASTag; Axis pools many FASTags into one wallet. The interface had to support both — without asking fleet owners to understand the difference.

DelFin normalises both into one FASTag management experience — bank switching, individual vs pooled balances, and the states that matter: active, inactive, hotlisted, low balance, plus recharge and resolution actions.

"The architecture changed. The user's mental model shouldn't have to."

12Reflection

What I learned building a new business line from zero.

01Design the system, not only the first feature.

Shared identity, vehicles and wallets are what make future services possible.

02Simplify the user's model, not the backend.

Banks can behave differently without pushing that complexity onto fleet owners.

03Speed requires better decisions, not less design.

A tight timeline meant prioritising critical journeys and validating continuously.

"DelFin taught me that designing a 0→1 product is as much about deciding what the product should become as it is about designing how it works."
