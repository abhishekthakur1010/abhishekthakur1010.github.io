A ticket lands. It's already 88 days overdue.

Amit, a frontline CS agent, opens this ticket. Under a one-line message sits a stack of private notes, each with a wall of email addresses. His next step is somewhere in there.

"THE ORDER IS DELAYED. THE CUSTOMER IS AVAILABLE. PLEASE DELIVER IT AS SOON AS POSSIBLE."

  2. 01:30No shipment status, so he opens the MAWB in HQ.HQ portal
  3. 03:10Found short at the Bengaluru DC. He looks up the Ops and Security contacts.Escalation Matrix

07Insight

Agents aren't trying to analyse. They're trying to fix.

That line from the floor became the brief. Every screen had to answer three questions.

Need 1

"What happened?"

Context, P0 facts and a summary at a glance.

Need 2

"What do I do next?"

The SLA and the next action, without scrolling.

Need 3

"Let me do it in one click."

Reply, note, forward and escalate without leaving the ticket.

"When a customer raises a ticket about a delayed shipment, I want to understand what's blocking it, so I can act before the SLA is breached."

Decision 2 · Escalation

Escalation became a button, not a research task

Escalate to Ops opens in the composer with the facility and TL and ATL contacts filled in. The agent adds context and sends.

Why: the system knows who to escalate to, so the agent shouldn't have to look it up.
Decision 3 · Private notes

Notes that reach whole teams

Notes turn yellow in the thread and address whole teams instead of individual people.

Why: the 30-address cap broke for big Ops teams, and a note must never look like a reply.
Decision 4 · SLA and context

Deadlines in words, the shipment beside the thread

"First response overdue by 5h." Shipment status, the current centre with its ageing and the consignee sit next to the thread.

Why: agents asked for ageing in hours, as in HQ. Now HQ can stay closed.
Decision 5 · Voice replies

Talk when typing is slower

Record a reply, check the transcript, send both.

Decision: show the transcript before sending, so the agent controls every word.
Decision 6 · AI summary

A summary instead of a scroll

Key ticket and package facts at a glance, rated by agents with a thumbs up or down.

Principle: AI assists, the agent decides.

10Shipped

What they asked for, and what V1 delivered.

I kept the floor's list open while designing. Here is what made V1, and what's next.

Asked for → in V1
  • Ageing in hours. The current centre shows "Ageing 24h".
  • Escalation inside the TDP. Facility and TL/ATL levels prefilled.
  • Contacts in the reply panel. TL and ATL addresses filled in.
  • Past the note cap. Notes address whole teams.
  • SLA up front. "First response overdue by 5h".
  • Clearer related tickets. AWB, status and ID inline.
Next phase
  • Image upload for damaged shipments
  • Dispatch and attempt count
  • Geo-fence verification indicator
  • Prepaid or COD indicator on tickets
  • Reassignment alerts and leave backup
  • Ticket download for audits
11Outcome

What changed on the floor.

Tickets that used to take about three days now close in a few hours. CS agents find the new TDP engaging and easier to learn: the right information is in one place, and escalation is one click instead of a lookup.

13Learnings

What I'd tell the next team.

Learning 1

Design for the new joiner

My real test was whether someone in their first week could close a ticket in one go.

Learning 2

Remove tools, not just clicks

Most lost time sat between screens. Bringing context in beat any layout change.

Learning 3

Keep the floor's list open

The team's own requests kept V1 honest about what it solved.
