What We’re Diving Into:
Express - Last Mile

This Case Study covers the Last Mile — Express’s fastest-paced layer, where UX drove faster deliveries, smoother dispatch, and better visibility.

🎯 Case Study Focus:
Last Mile (LM)

The final step where every second counts. Key systems we redesigned:

  • FE App 2.0: Clearer task flows, route guidance, fewer errors.
  • Partner App 2.0: Faster onboarding, payout visibility, delivery tracking.
  • New Digital Payment 2.0: QR-based payments to cut COD friction.
  • Delhivery Premier League: Gamified metrics to drive partner engagement.
  • LM Eye Dashboard: Real-time parcel tracking for DC teams.
  • LM Dispatch (ODX): Smarter trip planning and auto route assignment.
Problem Areas Across Core:
Last Mile Systems

Field visits, interviews, and system audits surfaced critical gaps across our core delivery tools — hurting both operations and experience.

Unclear flows, outdated UI, and poor route visibility slowed agents and hurt delivery success.

Manual, error-prone onboarding caused partner drop-offs. No self-serve option meant no scale.

Heavy cash reliance created risk; the digital payment flow was hidden and failed often.

FEs had no motivation beyond payouts — no way to reward performance or recognize progress.

Unclear flows and low feature awareness meant DC teams couldn’t act quickly on issues.

Challenges Along the Way:

Beyond user problems, we navigated real hurdles in our own design process:

  • No central intake: PMs pulled designers in without context or timelines.
  • Unclear priorities: A 12:2 PM-to-designer ratio kept the team reacting, not planning.
  • Lack of visibility: Designers couldn’t see what was coming next.
  • Blocked sprint planning: Our sprint rhythm stalled on internal pushback.
  • Tight windows: Often just 5 days to spec and ship complete designs #OneWeekDesignSprint.
✅ Quick Win: Design Sprint

I introduced a weekly Wednesday Design Sprint where PMs shared upcoming tasks and aligned on priorities.

Impact:
  • Created task visibility for designers
  • Improved delivery quality and planning
  • Reduced last-minute chaos and stress
  • Increased design output and clarity by almost 2×
Design Process:
Fast, Field-First and Scalable

The DS 2.0 redesign focused on discovery, engagement, and decision-making across key touchpoints.

Immersion - Where It All Started
Getting on the Ground

Before wireframes, we started where it matters most — on the ground.

  • I shadowed FEs at dispatch centers and delivered parcels myself.
  • Scanning parcels, app glitches, unclear addresses, on-the-go customers — we felt the friction firsthand.
  • This built empathy with FEs, TLs, DC managers and partners, grounding our design in real operations.
Delivery in Motion, Design in Mind
Research Methods

A mix of immersive and collaborative methods to observe, empathize, and validate — from field visits to feedback loops.

Shadowing FEs on live runs captured real pain points — from scanning under pressure to broken app flows — moving us beyond assumptions.

Benchmarked logistics apps (Amazon, Blue Dart, Shadowfax and others) to spot UX gaps.

Compared their Last Mile experiences across:
  • Task Flows: pickup/delivery clarity
  • Route Visibility: navigation guidance
  • Communication: consignee contact and status
  • Payments: digital options and confirmation
  • Performance Tracking: targets, payouts, progress

Mapped end-to-end FE and partner journeys to pinpoint delays, confusion, and handoff failures — the foundation for our redesigns.

Interviews with DC managers, TLs, and PMs surfaced operational gaps and backend complexity — shaping both internal tools and user-facing flows.

Product-Level Pain Points

Real gaps across our core tools — what slowed us down, confused users, or blocked scale.

Outdated UI with unclear task flows and no reschedule.
QR failed on low network; pickups needed manual handling.
No in-app support, feedback, or multi-language onboarding.

Long, complex onboarding drove high rider drop-off.
No self-onboarding; manual entry caused errors and delayed earning.

~85% of COD was cash, putting riders at risk.
QR was hard to find and often failed on network.
Too many options and weak confirmation kept QR usage low.

  • FEs had low motivation beyond payouts.
  • No scalable way to track or reward good performance — needed a fun system to boost morale.

Hard to navigate, with no parcel-level real-time visibility.
Low feature awareness and inconsistent usage across DCs.

Conclusion:
Driving Design Where It Matters Most

Redesigning the Last Mile wasn’t about updating screens — it was about giving clarity, control, and confidence to the people moving millions of parcels daily.

Grounded in on-ground immersion and operational empathy, we simplified tools across field execution, payments, onboarding, and engagement — designing for speed, scale, and service.

Personal Reflection

Great UX doesn’t start in a Figma file — it starts in the field. Shipping what truly works at scale is where design leadership makes its mark.
